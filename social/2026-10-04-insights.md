## LinkedIn

Most companies adding AI to customer service ask which tool to buy. The better question is who is trained to use it.

In customer care centres, the pattern is consistent. AI works best on predictable, repeated questions and on background work like drafting replies and summarising tickets. It works worst when a customer is upset, when money or privacy is involved, or when nobody can reach a person.

The teams that get value share three habits:
1. They write approved answers before automating anything.
2. They define the exact moment a human takes over.
3. They train agents to review and edit AI drafts, not just send them.

The takeaway for leaders: the technology is the easy part. Training your people to use it with empathy and accuracy is what protects customer trust.
