## LinkedIn

Your team has an AI tool. Are they getting answers they can actually use?

Most do not, and the reason is rarely the technology. It is the instruction. Vague prompts produce vague drafts that someone has to rewrite.

The method I teach has four parts:
1. Role: tell AI who it is.
2. Goal: say exactly what you need.
3. Context: tone, policy, audience, and what it must not say.
4. Format: length and shape of the answer.

For customer care teams, this means faster draft replies, clearer ticket summaries and calmer rewrites of harsh messages, with a human still reviewing every reply. For marketing teams, it means hooks written for a named audience and one clear goal.

Three habits protect your brand: never paste private customer data into unapproved tools, check every fact, and refine the first draft instead of sending it.

The takeaway for leaders: the tool is the easy part. Training your people to use it well is what turns AI into saved hours.
